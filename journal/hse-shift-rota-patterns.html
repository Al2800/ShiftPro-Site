HSE shift rota patterns: nursing, NCHDs, and working time limits

An HSE rota is the timetable of who is working in Ireland's public health service, and when. This is the Health Service Executive in Ireland, not the UK NHS. Your start and finish are set where you work. A rota, in plain terms, is the list of shifts and days off: see what a work rota is.

The figures below are from the HSE handbook, the June 2026 terms and conditions, the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997, and the nurse and doctor notes linked in each section. They were checked on 20 November 2026. Your contract can be more specific than this page.

When do hospital shifts change in Ireland?

There is no national time when hospital shifts change in Ireland. The HSE Employee Handbook 2026 says your hours of work are in your contract, and your times of attendance are as notified by your line manager. You are expected to arrive for the start time agreed with your manager, and to stay until the agreed or rostered finish.

Some services run longer than a 9 to 5 day. HR Circular 003/2009 is about delivering services outside that old pattern, typically from 8am to 8pm, and at weekends. HR Circular 009/2025 uses the same 8am to 8pm span, and says Injury Units should offer a consistent 7-day service from 8am to 8pm. That is an extended service day. It is not a clock time when every ward hands over. The 2025 circular also says extended hours differ from site to site, because they follow local demand. Read the roster you were given.

Shift change times in the UK are a different system. They are covered in NHS shift rota patterns.

12-hour shifts and 5 over 7 rosters

A 12-hour shift on an HSE rota is a rostered duty of 12 hours. The start and the finish still come from your own roster. Letters on that roster are local too. A code such as LD means whatever your ward key says it means. The HSE does not publish one national list of shift letters. On an NHS rota, LD is usually a long day, which the NHS shift patterns page explains. Do not copy that meaning onto an Irish roster.

Where the days are different lengths, annual leave is counted in hours. The HSE terms and conditions (June 2026) divide the normal weekly hours for the grade by 5, then multiply by the number of leave days. The worked example is a 39-hour week with 24 days of leave: 24 times 7.8 hours is 187.2 hours. If you take annual leave on a day you would have been rostered for a 12-hour shift, 12 hours come off that balance. Nursing and midwifery overtime uses a 37.5-hour divisor, so the 39-hour example is not every grade.

A 5 over 7 roster is five working days out of any seven, so Saturdays and Sundays come round. The same June 2026 terms set the public holiday leave that goes with it:

  • Nursing and midwifery staff on 5 over 7 have a contractual extra 10 days of leave, pro-rated for part-time staff, in lieu of being liable to work public holidays. If you are rostered on the public holiday, you are also paid single time extra for each hour worked.
  • NCHDs on 5 over 7 get 10 working days in lieu, counted at 7.8 hours a day. Five days fall due from the second Monday in January to the second Monday in July, and five from the second Monday in July to the second Monday in January. Hours worked on the public holiday attract single time extra.

For nurses and midwives, INMO's overtime note (the June 2026 leaflet linked from that page) says that where a 12-hour shift applies, extra hours worked within the span of that shift are paid at flat time. Hours asked for outside the span of a normal shift are treated as overtime, and the rate depends on the day. A repeating pattern whose days off move is a rolling rota.

Night duty, twilight and other unsocial-hour payments

HSE night duty, the payment, is not the same thing as a night worker in the Act.

For pay, the June 2026 terms and the HSE allowances page say night duty is normally hours worked between 8pm and 8am. The premium is time plus a quarter, and only if you are rostered through the night: at least three hours between midnight and 7am. A shift from 4pm to midnight does not get it. On a Sunday or a public holiday, that premium is time plus a quarter on basic salary only. It does not include the unsocial-hours allowance for that shift. NCHD night duty is separate. The June 2026 terms point to HR Circular 003/2014, and say the NCHD contract can extend the premium from 5pm to 8am when the duty starts at 5pm and runs through the night.

INMO's premium page, for nurses and midwives, agrees the usual window (8pm to 8am, with at least three hours between midnight and 7am, at time plus a quarter). Under its Sunday heading it also describes rostered night duty as time plus a quarter plus time. That is a different description from the HSE page. Check the line on your payslip against your grade.

The legal test is shorter. Section 16 of the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 defines night time as midnight to 7am. You are a night worker if you normally work at least 3 hours of your daily working time in that window, and if your night hours for the year are at least half of your total hours. The HSE page on night and shift workers uses that same test. Being paid a premium for an 8pm to 8am shift does not, on its own, make you a night worker.

Twilight is time plus one sixth. The June 2026 terms say nurses and midwives, support staff, and social care workers and leaders who are already eligible, and who are rostered between 6pm and 8pm or until the end of the day shift, can be paid it. Some locations already pay time and a sixth to other grades. INMO says the 6pm to 8pm payment applies to nurses and midwives, and that time plus one sixth can also cover 8pm to midnight unless a local alternative under HSE Circular 003/2012 is in place. The HSE allowances page describes twilight for approved grades as the time from 8pm to the end of the day shift, and the 6pm to 8pm payment for nurses and midwives in acute hospitals and in social care. If your hours sit across both descriptions, ask your line manager which one your grade uses.

On a 5 over 7 roster, the June 2026 terms and the allowances page say:

  • Saturday is a fixed allowance, whatever the number of hours. The HSE terms do not print a euro amount. INMO's June 2026 premium note lists €19.20 for a nurse or midwife scheduled to work that Saturday. If the public holiday falls on a Saturday, the Saturday allowance is not paid as well. The public holiday premium applies instead.
  • Sunday is single time extra for each hour worked, which is double time.
  • A public holiday is the same: single time extra for each hour.

On-call or standby is usually a fixed payment for being available. If you are called out, a further payment is possible. The amount depends on the grade, so the allowances page tells you to check with your line manager. Sleepover, in the June 2026 terms, is for residential social care that runs 24 hours a day. It is a continuous period of 8 hours or more, usually between 11pm and 8am, and the hourly rate follows the adult minimum wage in the Department of Health scales. It moves when that rate moves.

Part-time staff get overtime rates only after they have worked more than the full-time weekly hours for the grade. Hours up to that full-time week are paid at flat time, or given as time off in lieu. The terms add one exception: if you are kept on past the end of your shift for something that was not planned, overtime can apply even if you have not yet reached the full-time week.

Rest breaks and the 48-hour average week

The Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 sets the floor. The Workplace Relations Commission sets it out in plain language. A contract can give you more rest. These are the statutory rules:

  • Daily rest is 11 consecutive hours in each 24-hour period (section 11).
  • Weekly rest is at least 24 consecutive hours in each period of 7 days, and it is timed so that it follows the daily rest (section 13). The employer can instead give two 24-hour rests in the next week. Where the work is organised in a way that justifies it, the weekly rest need not start straight after the daily rest.
  • Breaks are at least 15 minutes when the working period is more than 4 hours and 30 minutes, and at least 30 minutes when it is more than 6 hours. The 30 minutes may include the 15. A break at the end of the working day does not count (section 12). The WRC says payment for breaks is not a statutory entitlement.
  • Weekly working time must not average more than 48 hours. The reference period is no longer than 4 months, or 6 months in the seasonal and continuity cases the Act lists, or up to 12 months where a collective agreement approved by the Labour Court sets that longer period (section 15). The WRC describes the same three periods: 4, 6 or 12 months.

Night work has its own cap in section 16. A night worker must not average more than 8 hours in each 24-hour period. That average is taken over a reference period of up to 2 months, or longer if a Labour Court-approved collective agreement says so. The WRC describes this limit as 48 hours a week, averaged over 2 months. Where the work involves special hazards or a heavy physical or mental strain, the limit is a hard 8 hours in a 24-hour period in which the night work is done. It is not an average.

NCHD rotas, on-call and the EWTD

Non-consultant hospital doctors (NCHDs) have rostering rules on top of the Act. There is still no national time when doctors change shift. HR Circular 028/2017 says start and finish follow clinics, theatre, ward rounds and handover, and that each handover is at least 30 minutes.

The same circular explains working time for the European Working Time Directive. On-site on-call counts, because you are at the workplace and at the employer's disposal. Off-site on-call does not count unless you are required to perform a service while you are off site. Time spent on site when you are off call does not count.

The circular restates the NCHD Contract 2010 rostering limits:

  • No more than 24 consecutive hours on site.
  • A 24-hour on-site stretch no more often than 1 in 5, except in exceptional circumstances.
  • A handover of at least 30 minutes between shifts.
  • No split shifts. A split shift is a working day broken into two or more separate blocks.
  • At least 2 weeks' notice for the first issue of a roster.
  • A 15-minute break after 4 hours 30 minutes, or a 30-minute break after 6 hours. The circular says clinical work means those breaks are often taken when the work allows, and that the hospital should record that they were given.

Rest in that circular follows the 11-hour daily rest. The weekly or fortnightly rest is 35 hours once a week (11 hours followed by 24), or 35 hours twice a fortnight, or 59 hours once a fortnight (11 hours followed by 48). If a break or a rest is missed, compensatory rest of the same length is due before the next rostered period. It can be interrupted if you are called, but it still has to add up.

The IMO NCHD agreement note of 4 January 2023 says the agreement came into force on 1 January 2023. The rostering points in that note are:

  • The 48-hour average week is checked over 3 months. The note says the previous compliance period was 6 months.
  • After 10 days in a row you get a rest day. After 11 you get two. Those can be your normal days off. They are there to break the run. If they are not given straight afterwards, they become compensatory rest, due within 4 weeks, on top of your ordinary rest, or else they are paid.
  • No consecutive weekends. That includes off-site call.
  • In any 14 days, no more than four 13-hour days, and no more than two 24-hour shifts. The hours of those shifts together must not go over 96, so four 13-hour days plus two 24-hour shifts in the same fortnight is over the line. A third 24-hour shift is allowed only if you agree, and it brings an extra compensatory rest day.
  • Around a 24-hour shift you must be rostered off for the 11 hours before it and the 11 hours after it.
  • If you are moving site, you get 24 hours off before the new rotation. The note says every effort should be made to make that a full 48 hours.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions about HSE shift patterns

Direct answers on hospital shift changes in Ireland, HSE night duty, rest and the 48-hour week, and 12-hour shifts.

When do hospital shifts change in Ireland?

There is no single national time when hospital shifts change in Ireland. The HSE Employee Handbook 2026 says your times of attendance are as notified by your line manager, so check the roster for your own ward or unit. An 8am to 8pm span appears in the extended working day arrangements, including a 7-day 8am to 8pm service for Injury Units, but that is how long a service can be open. It is not a ward handover time.

What counts as night duty in the HSE?

HSE night duty is normally hours worked between 8pm and 8am, and only if you work at least 3 hours between midnight and 7am. Those hours pay time plus a quarter. A shift from 4pm to midnight does not qualify. The legal definition of a night worker is narrower: night time is midnight to 7am, and a night worker normally works at least 3 hours then and at least half of their hours for the year in that window.

What rest and weekly-hour limits apply?

Under the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 you get 11 consecutive hours of rest in each 24 hours, and 24 consecutive hours of weekly rest that follow that daily rest. You also get a 15-minute break after more than 4 hours 30 minutes, and a 30-minute break after more than 6 hours, which may include the first break. Payment for breaks is not a statutory entitlement. The average week must not go over 48 hours, normally measured over 4 months.

What is a 12-hour shift on an HSE rota?

A 12-hour shift is a rostered duty of 12 hours. Start and finish times are set locally, not by a national HSE clock. If you take annual leave on a day you were rostered for 12 hours, 12 hours come off your leave. Letters on the roster, such as LD, are local codes. The HSE does not publish them as a national standard.

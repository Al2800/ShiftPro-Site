Shift rota patterns and templates explained

Searching for shift rota templates often leads to generic spreadsheets with broken formulas. Rather than wrestling with clunky downloads, understanding the proven mathematical patterns behind shift work makes designing or living with a rota much easier.

Every 24/7 or multi-shift operation relies on standard rotation templates that have been tested over decades. Here is how the most popular shift rota patterns work in practice, including their cycles, rest days, and trade-offs.

1. The 4-on, 4-off pattern (4 on 4 off)

Popular across manufacturing, logistics, ambulance trusts, and police services, 4-on 4-off is based on 12-hour shifts repeating over an 8-day cycle:

  • How it works: Work 2 Day shifts (12h) → 2 Night shifts (12h) → 4 consecutive Rest days OFF. Alternatively, 4 consecutive Days → 4 Days OFF, alternating with 4 Nights → 4 OFF.
  • The pros: Every block of work is rewarded with an uninterrupted 4-day weekend. With 12-hour shifts, you work fewer total days in the year (around 182 shifts per year instead of ~230).
  • The cons: Because the cycle is 8 days long, your shifts advance by one day each week. You work different days of the week every single cycle, meaning weekends off only coincide periodically. Learn how rolling cycles work and how to calculate future days off in our guide to what is a rolling rota.

2. The Continental shift pattern

The Continental pattern is a fast-rotating system originally popularised in European continuous processing industries. It usually operates over a 28-day cycle across 4 teams:

  • How it works: 2 Morning shifts (e.g. 07:00–15:00) → 2 Afternoon/Late shifts (15:00–23:00) → 3 Night shifts (23:00–07:00) → 2 days Rest → 2 Mornings → 3 Afternoons → 2 Nights → 3 days Rest.
  • The pros: Shifts rotate forward rapidly, meaning you never spend more than 2 or 3 consecutive days on any single shift type. Fatigue from night shifts does not build up over a long continuous block.
  • The cons: Quick changeovers leave short recovery windows between morning and night blocks. It takes discipline to manage sleep with such rapid changes.

3. The DuPont shift pattern

Developed by the chemical company DuPont, this 12-hour schedule operates over a 4-week (28-day) cycle and is famous for providing a full 7-day stretch off work every month:

  • How it works: 4 consecutive night shifts → 3 days off → 3 day shifts → 1 day off → 3 night shifts → 3 days off → 4 day shifts → 7 consecutive days off.
  • The pros: That 7-day break allows shift workers to take genuine vacations and recharge without booking any annual leave.
  • The cons: Working 4 consecutive 12-hour night shifts can be physically punishing, and the single day off between shift blocks is barely enough to adjust sleep.

4. The 2-2-3 (Pitman) pattern

Common in emergency dispatch, security, and healthcare, the Pitman pattern works over a 14-day cycle with two 12-hour teams:

  • How it works: 2 Days ON → 2 Days OFF → 3 Days ON → 2 Days OFF → 2 Days ON → 3 Days OFF.
  • The pros: You never work more than 3 consecutive days, and every other weekend is a guaranteed 3-day weekend (Friday, Saturday, Sunday).
  • The cons: 12-hour shifts require long working days, and switching between on and off every two days can feel like a rollercoaster.
Template comparison at a glance

Looking for long recovery blocks? Consider 4-on 4-off or DuPont. Looking for guaranteed alternate weekends? The 2-2-3 Pitman is usually the best fit. Prefer short blocks so nights never drag on? Look at the Continental pattern.

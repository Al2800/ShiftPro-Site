Common questions

Frequently asked questions about rotas

Direct answers to essential questions on shift rotas, work rotas, rolling cycles, and UK scheduling rules.

What is a shift rota?

A shift rota is a recurring schedule that coordinates continuous, multi-crew coverage across mornings, evenings, weekends, or overnight hours. It divides operations into defined shifts (such as earlies, lates, and nights) with structured handover windows so a site or service never closes unmanned. Learn more in our guide on how to make a shift rota.

What is a work rota?

A work rota is a planned timetable showing which staff members work on specific calendar days to cover business opening hours. It is commonly used in retail, hospitality, clinics, and offices where staffing expands or contracts around customer footfall and statutory rest periods. Read our complete guide to what a work rota is in the UK.

What is the difference between a shift rota and a work rota?

A work rota matches staff hours to daytime business opening times, whereas a shift rota manages round-the-clock handover cycles and unsocial working hours. Shift rotas require strict compliance with UK night work limits and relief factor planning to cover continuous 24/7 or 16/7 services. Read our full comparison of shift rota vs work rota.

How do I make a rota?

To make a rota, calculate your baseline staffing requirement first, lock in approved leave and legal rest rules, and assign shifts across a balanced repeating pattern. Once your template is filled, stress-test it against short-notice sick leave and publish it at least two to three weeks early. Follow our 5-step walkthrough on how to make a work rota.

What is a rolling rota?

A rolling rota is a repeating shift pattern that advances across calendar days rather than locking to set weekdays, causing your working days and rest breaks to roll forward each week. Popular UK cycles like 4-on 4-off take 8 days to complete, meaning your shifts and rest days start one day later every week. Check our guide on what a rolling rota is to learn how to predict your rest days months ahead.

What is a rota planner?

A rota planner is either software used by managers to draft team schedules, or a personal app used by workers to track shifts, log actual hours, and check pay. While team planners manage shift bidding and leave approvals, personal planners like ShiftPro help individual shift workers track repeating patterns, overtime, and unsocial pay privately on iPhone.

How do you make a shift rota for 24/7 coverage?

To make a shift rota for 24/7 coverage, calculate your total weekly operational hours (168 hours for round-the-clock service) and divide by shift lengths to find the required shifts per week. Add a relief factor of 20% to 25% to cover annual leave, training, and sickness, then distribute shifts across at least four or five distinct crews using forward-rotating patterns. Read our complete guide on how to make a shift rota.

What UK rest rules apply to a work rota?

Under the UK Working Time Regulations 1998, a work rota must provide at least 11 consecutive hours of daily rest between shifts, 24 uninterrupted hours of weekly rest (or 48 hours every fortnight), and a 20-minute break during shifts longer than six hours. Rotas must also average no more than 48 hours per week across a 17-week reference period unless staff have opted out. See our full breakdown of legal limits in what is a work rota.

How do you make a fair staff rota?

To make a fair staff rota, map required shift coverage before assigning names, apply fixed rules like contracted hours and legal rest breaks first, and rotate weekend and night duties equally across the whole team. Publishing the finished schedule at least two to three weeks in advance gives staff predictable notice and cuts last-minute swap requests. Follow our practical rules in how to make a fair staff rota.

How do NHS shift rotas work?

NHS shift rotas typically use 12.5-hour long days and night shifts on acute hospital wards, or a three-shift rotation of earlies, lates, and nights across community services. Full-time clinical staff work an average of 37.5 hours per week under Agenda for Change contracts, with enhanced pay for unsocial night, weekend, and public holiday hours.

    What unsocial hours are

    Unsocial hours are the evenings, nights, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays inside the standard working week. Agenda for Change pays a percentage on top of basic pay for those hours. A search for unsociable hours means the same payments. The handbook calls them unsocial hours.

    In England, section 2 has applied since 1 July 2018. The percentages below are the ones the handbook shows from 1 April 2020 onwards.

    Pay bandWeekday nights (20:00 to 06:00) and SaturdaysSundays and public holidays

    Band 2time plus 41%time plus 83%

    The night and Saturday rate covers all of Saturday, midnight to midnight, and weekday time between 8pm and 6am (20:00 to 06:00). The Sunday and public holiday rate covers the whole of those days, also midnight to midnight. Each hour is paid at one of those rates.

    One more rule applies to a continuous evening or night shift on a weekday that is not a public holiday. If more than half of that shift falls between 8pm and 6am, the Saturday and night percentage applies to the whole shift. Only hours inside that window count towards the half.

    Part-time staff working fewer than 37.5 hours a week still receive these payments. The percentage is worked out on basic pay, including any long-term recruitment and retention premium. Short-term recruitment and retention premiums, high cost area supplements and other payments are excluded.

    Bands 4 to 9 share one row, which is why the calculator groups them. Overtime stops at band 7, and the next section covers it. The Wales and Northern Ireland sections of the same handbook show these same percentages. Scotland's section 2 uses different percentages for bands 1 to 3. This calculator uses the England rates, and its bank holidays are the England and Wales dates.

    NHS rotas that use these nights and long days are described in our guide to NHS shift rota patterns.

    NHS overtime pay

    Overtime means hours worked above 37.5 in a week. Section 10 sets the full-time week at 37.5 hours, excluding meal breaks.

    Staff in pay bands 1 to 7 are eligible. The rate is time and a half for all overtime, and double time for work on a general public holiday. It is one rate across those bands.

    Staff in pay bands 8 and 9 are not entitled to overtime payments. Pay band 8 is divided into 8a, 8b, 8c and 8d, so the rule includes band 8a and above.

    Your line manager or team leader has to agree that the extra work is overtime. You can ask for time off in lieu instead of payment. Time off in lieu is plain time. If operational reasons mean you cannot take it within three months, the hours are paid at the overtime rate.

    Part-time staff are paid plain time for extra hours until that week goes over 37.5. From that point, bands 1 to 7 are paid at the overtime rates above.

    The overtime hourly rate is basic pay plus any long-term recruitment and retention premium. Section 4 says a high cost area supplement does not count as basic pay for overtime or for unsocial hours.

    Source: section 3 of the NHS Terms and Conditions of Service Handbook, paragraphs 3.1 to 3.7, checked 25 November 2026. Scotland publishes its own handbook, so NHS Scotland staff should check that copy.

    How overtime and unsocial hours fit together

    Unsocial hours payments apply to time inside the standard week. Section 2 says any extra time above standard hours is overtime, and section 3 applies.

    The unsocial hours percentage is left out of the overtime sum. The NHS Employers unsocial hours page says the same thing: overtime worked in unsocial hours is paid at time and a half, or at double time on a general public holiday.

    A public holiday can fall under either rule. Hours inside the standard week use the Sunday and public holiday percentage in the table. Hours that are overtime, for bands 1 to 7, are paid at double time on a general public holiday.

    The totals on this page are the unsocial hours estimate for the shifts you enter. The calculator does not know your contracted hours for the week, so every hour you enter stays on the unsocial hours rates.

    How the calculator works

    Each shift that crosses midnight is split at midnight. A Saturday night that runs into Sunday is the Saturday rate until 00:00 and the Sunday rate after it.

    Bank holidays for England and Wales are taken from the GOV.UK bank holiday list. The dates in the page run from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2028. Check any date outside that range yourself. The tool can treat a public holiday outside that range as an ordinary weekday.

    The more-than-half rule runs only on a weekday shift that does not start on a public holiday. Saturday, Sunday and public holiday minutes keep the rates in the table.

    An unpaid break is shared across the shift in proportion to its unsocial and daytime hours. The tool does not know the clock time of the break.

    Worked example

    £17.50 in the hourly rate box is an example figure you can replace. It is not a band 5 pay point from the current pay circular.

    A band 5 shift uses the bands 4 to 9 night rate, time plus 30%. Take a Wednesday night from 19:30 to 08:00 with a 30-minute unpaid break, and use £17.50 as the rate you type in. Ten of the 12.5 hours fall between 20:00 and 06:00, so more than half the shift is unsocial. All 12 paid hours get the 30% enhancement: 12 × £17.50 × 30% = £63.00, on top of £210.00 basic pay.

    NHS overtime and unsocial hours questions

    What is the NHS overtime rate?

    The NHS overtime rate for pay bands 1 to 7 is time and a half, and double time for work on a general public holiday. Overtime is hours above 37.5 in a week, excluding meal breaks, agreed with your line manager or team leader. Pay bands 8 and 9 are not entitled to overtime payments. Band 8 is split into 8a, 8b, 8c and 8d, so band 8a and above are included. Part-time staff are paid plain time for extra hours until the week passes 37.5. The unsocial hours percentage is left out of the overtime calculation.

    How much is NHS bank holiday pay?

    Hours on a public holiday inside the standard week are paid at the Sunday and public holiday rate: time plus 60% for bands 4 to 9, time plus 94% for band 1, time plus 83% for band 2, and time plus 69% for band 3. Where those hours are overtime for bands 1 to 7, a general public holiday is paid at double time. The percentage and the overtime rate are separate. The calculator uses the England and Wales bank holiday dates published by GOV.UK, for 2025 to 2028. Type in your own hourly rate for an estimate of the unsocial hours part.

    What are unsocial hours?

    Unsocial hours are evenings, nights, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays worked inside the standard NHS week, paid as a percentage on top of basic pay. Agenda for Change calls them unsocial hours. A search for unsociable hours means the same payments. In England, the lower percentage covers all of Saturday (midnight to midnight) and weekday time between 8pm and 6am. The higher percentage covers all of Sunday and public holidays (midnight to midnight). Section 2 in England has applied since 1 July 2018, and the table on this page is the handbook table from 1 April 2020 onwards.

    Do band 5 nurses get paid more for night shifts?

    Yes, a band 5 nurse is in the bands 4 to 9 group, so weekday time between 8pm and 6am is paid at time plus 30% during the standard week. Saturday uses the same 30%. Sunday and public holidays are time plus 60%. If more than half of a continuous weekday night shift falls between 8pm and 6am, the whole shift gets the 30% rate. Hours above 37.5 in the week are overtime at time and a half, or double time on a general public holiday, and the 30% is left out of that overtime sum.

    What this calculator does not cover

    The tool estimates unsocial hours enhancements on the shifts you enter. Overtime, on-call and high cost area supplements are handled separately.

    Some ambulance staff are still paid under Annex 5. Section 2 is the scheme this calculator uses. In England, staff who start in an ambulance trust on or after 1 September 2018, and staff who change role, move to section 2. Staff who remain on Annex 5 receive a percentage of basic salary based on average unsocial hours, up to 25% for bands 1 to 7 and 10% for bands 8 and above. The Annex 5 clock times differ. For bands 1 to 7 they include weekday time before 7am and after 7pm. Annex 4 points to older on-call arrangements, separate from the percentage table above. The same hours cannot be paid as unsocial hours and as on-call.

    Resident doctors are on a different contract. Scotland publishes its own handbook, and some of these rules differ there. Your trust may also have a local agreement.
